# Build my plan: a personal training plan from the whole catalogue

Status: approved by Emil 2026-11-10, with sex added to the person model. Replaces T-017's "Find a program for me" (which only
picked one of the 7 templates).

## Intent

A friend answers a few questions about their body, experience, goal, time and equipment. The app
shows "Building the best plan for you", then a plan made for them from all 5,632 catalogue
exercises: the split, the exercises, sets, reps, effort and rest for 8 weeks, with a plain
rationale saying why each choice was made. The choices come from our own written model built on
the research Emil supplied (docs/emails-gym-planning/research, mainly B and D), not from a stock
template and not from an AI.

Emil's two reference friends, who must come out clearly different:

- **Friend A:** 176 cm, 75 kg, trains 2 to 4 times a week, quite strong; wants muscle while losing
  some fat.
- **Friend B:** 187 cm, 68 kg, barely trains; needs big compound lifts to gain weight and muscle.

## Goals

- A plan unique to the answers, drawn from the whole catalogue, that a coach would call sensible.
- A rationale in plain words for the split, the exercises, the volume, the reps and effort, and the
  first weeks, each line traceable to a rule in the model.
- One named, versioned model (`EG Plan Model v1`) whose rules live in one place, with tests, that
  Emil's coach friend can read and change.
- The plan becomes ordinary routines plus a programme (the T-014/T-016 machinery), so the workout
  screen, progression, history, Home and reminders work as they do.

## Non-goals

- Nutrition and supplements. Next project, after Emil's research; this one stores the body details
  so it can reuse them.
- AI or LLM generation. Every number comes from the rules.
- Adapting the plan from week to week by itself (the existing progression engine does that).
- Body-fat estimates, calorie targets, medical or injury rehab programs.

## Anti-goals (failure even if it technically works)

- Odd exercises: stretches, "v. 3", "side pov", "(left)", circus moves, anything a coach would
  laugh at, chosen as a main lift.
- A plan that doesn't fit the stated days or minutes, or needs equipment they don't have.
- Friend A and Friend B getting near-identical plans.
- Body numbers judged or shamed ("you're underweight") instead of used quietly.
- Pretending the loading screen does more than it does, or claiming medical validity.

## Constraints

- Plain JS, pure functions in `frontend/src/lib/` with tests beside them (CONTRIBUTING: anything that
  decides what you lift is a pure helper with a unit test). No new dependencies. English through
  `t()`, plain, no em dashes. 44 px targets, existing components and tokens.
- The repo is public (AGPL); the model's rules are visible (Emil: fine).
- Never touch or feed exercise media; the index is built from the catalogue's JSON only.
- Deterministic: the same answers and model version always give the same plan; "Shuffle" changes
  only a seed.

## Decided

- **Questions** (Emil, "sounds about right"): goal (build muscle / build muscle and lose fat / gain
  weight and muscle / get stronger / general fitness / strength and cardio); height, weight, age,
  sex; experience (time trained) plus simple ability checks (push-ups, bodyweight squats, has done
  barbell lifts); days a week; minutes per session; equipment (full gym, dumbbells, none, or the
  active gym profile's own list); optional focus areas (chest, back, shoulders, arms, glutes, legs,
  core); injuries or areas to go easy on (knee, shoulder, lower back, wrist, neck); the health
  question.
- **Health yes:** no plan; the existing "talk to a doctor or physiotherapist" screen.
- **Injuries:** steer exercise choice away from stressing that area (never "no plan").
- **Loading screen then plan with rationale** (Emil).
- **The 7 programs stay** as templates on the Programs screen.
- **Rules visible in code**, no secrecy (Emil).
- **Nutrition next, separately** (Emil).

## How it works (the model)

Three pure stages, each its own file, plus the data index.

### 1. The exercise index (`library/exercise-index/` → generated `frontend/src/lib/exercise-index-data.js`)

Our own layer over the catalogue, generated by a script (like `scripts/library/build.mjs`), with a
`--check` in `scripts/fork/check.sh`. For each exercise it holds:

- `pattern`: squat, hinge, lunge (single-leg), horizontal push, vertical push, horizontal pull,
  vertical pull, hip extension, knee flexion, knee extension, calf, elbow flexion, elbow extension,
  lateral raise, rear delt, chest fly, core anti-extension, core rotation, carry, or `null`.
  Inferred from target, body part, secondary muscles and name keywords, plus a hand-kept overrides
  file.
- `compound`: true when it trains several large muscles through two joints or more (secondary
  muscles plus pattern).
- `skill`: 1 to 3 (machine or supported = 1, free-weight dumbbell/cable = 2, barbell compound,
  unilateral free-weight or gymnastic = 3).
- `tier`: `core` (a hand-reviewed list of a few hundred standard exercises: the plain barbell,
  dumbbell, cable, machine and body-weight versions people know), `common` (clean names, eligible),
  `variant` (eligible only as a swap), `excluded` (stretching, mobility, yoga, pilates, rehab,
  combat; names with "v. N", "pov", "(left)/(right)", holds already covered; anything a rule flags).
- `stress`: the areas it loads hard (knee, shoulder, lower back, wrist, neck), from pattern and
  name rules plus overrides, for the injury filter.

The core list starts from the exercises already used by the starter plans, the 7 programs'
mapping and `lib/exercise-substitutes.js` families, then fills each pattern × equipment cell with
its standard exercise. It is the most important hand-curated input and goes in the coach's review.

### 2. The person (`lib/plan-person.js`)

From the answers, a small profile with the reason for each value:

- **Training level:** novice / intermediate / advanced, from time trained, raised one step by the
  ability checks (someone who "doesn't go" but can do 20 push-ups starts as novice-plus, not a
  beginner on machines only), capped by the answers.
- **Body:** BMI band from height and weight (under 18.5, 18.5 to 25, 25 to 30, over 30). Used only
  to steer emphasis (gaining: more compound volume; losing: keep heavy work, add easy cardio
  sessions or steps), never shown as a label.
- **Recovery:** age over 50 or a novice: one fewer hard set per exercise in weeks 1 to 2 and longer
  rest on compounds.
- **Sex** (Emil: it should count). Muscle and strength gains respond to the same training at
  similar rates in women and men (research A/B, ACSM 2026), so sex never changes the safety rules
  or picks a different "kind" of plan. It changes defaults, each overridable and named in the
  rationale:
  - **Default emphasis:** with no focus areas chosen, a female profile gets glutes and legs as the
    default focus (one extra lower-body slot or set per week); a male profile gets none by default.
    Choosing focus areas replaces the default.
  - **Rest and volume:** women tend to recover faster between sets at the same relative effort,
    so isolation rest goes to the low end (60 s) and accessory sets may take one more set within
    the level's weekly range.
  - **Upper-body starting point:** smaller upper-body jumps (the smallest increment the equipment
    allows) and assisted or machine versions first for vertical pulls and dips-style pushes when
    the ability checks show they can't yet do a few bodyweight reps.
  - "Prefer not to say" uses the neutral defaults.
- **Goal emphasis:** hypertrophy, recomposition, weight gain, strength, general, hybrid.

### 3. The plan rules (`lib/plan-model.js`, the one file the coach reviews)

Tables, each row with an id and a one-line reason that the rationale reuses (research B unless
marked):

- **Split by days and level:** 2 days full body A/B; 3 days full body A/B/C (novice, general,
  gain) or upper/lower/full (intermediate+); 4 days upper/lower ×2; 5 days upper/lower/push/pull/legs
  or PPL + upper/lower. Each muscle trained at least twice a week (B "2+ broad muscle exposures").
- **Session slots:** each session type is an ordered list of pattern slots (for example lower:
  squat, hinge, lunge, knee flexion, calf, core). Compounds and skill-heavy lifts first. Focus
  areas add a slot or a set to that area. The number of slots is capped by minutes per session
  using the T-015 time estimate (8 min warm-up, 40 s per set, the prescribed rest).
- **Weekly sets per muscle:** novice 6 to 10, intermediate 10 to 16, advanced 12 to 20 (Pelland
  2026, diminishing returns); 2 to 4 sets per exercise; the time cap wins over the volume target.
- **Reps:** strength goal: 3 to 6 on the main lifts, 6 to 10 accessories. Hypertrophy, recomp,
  gain: 6 to 10 compounds, 10 to 15 isolation. General: 8 to 12. Holds in seconds.
- **Effort (RIR):** weeks 1 to 2: 3 to 4 for novices, 2 to 3 otherwise; then 1 to 3 for
  hypertrophy, 2 to 3 on strength lifts; week 8 lighter (one fewer set, +1 RIR).
- **Rest:** 2 to 3 min compounds, 60 to 90 s isolation (B "rest protects output").
- **Cardio:** recomp and fat-loss: two 20 to 30 min easy sessions or a daily steps note;
  hybrid: as hybrid-4's corrected sessions; gaining: none beyond warm-ups.
- **8 weeks:** week 1 to 2 baseline, 3 to 7 the full sets, 8 lighter; progression within the
  weeks is the app's existing double progression.

### 4. Choosing exercises (`lib/plan-build.js`)

For each slot, score every eligible exercise and take the best, deterministically:

- **Must:** pattern matches the slot; equipment available; not `excluded`; not stressing an area
  the person flagged; skill ≤ level + 1 for main lifts.
- **Score** (weights in `plan-model.js`): core +30, common +10; compound +15 when the goal is gain,
  strength or the person is novice; skill matching level +10 (novice: machines and dumbbells first,
  strength goal: barbell first); exercises they've logged before +10 (familiar beats novel);
  favourites +10; variety −20 for the same equipment and pattern already in the week, −40 for the
  same exercise twice in one session.
- **Ties** broken by a seeded order, so "Shuffle" gives a different but equally good pick and the
  same seed always gives the same plan.

Output: the T-016 shape (templates with items, an 8-week plan), plus `rationale: [{ rule, text }]`
and `model: { name: 'EG Plan Model', version: 1, seed }`, so the existing start code turns it into
routines and a programme with `src: { model, version, seed }`.

## The screens

- **Programs screen:** "Build my plan" replaces "Find a program for me" at the top; the 7 programs
  below under "Templates". (My call: T-017's matcher screen and `program-match.js` are removed,
  since nothing uses them; the templates are browsed with the filters.)
- **Questions:** the T-017 one-per-screen flow, extended with the new questions; height and weight
  in the profile's units (cm/in, kg/lb); weight prefilled from the latest weigh-in.
- **Building:** "Building the best plan for you" with three honest steps ticking off (Choosing
  your split, Picking your exercises, Setting sets, reps and rest). The work takes milliseconds; the
  screen shows for about two seconds so the steps can be read (my call, Emil asked for this
  moment).
- **Your plan:** name ("Your plan: muscle and fat loss, 4 days"), the rationale first (5 to 8 short
  lines, each expandable to the rule and its source), then sessions and weeks as on the program
  detail page. Actions: **Start plan**, **Save to my routines**, **Shuffle exercises**, **Swap**
  on any exercise (opens the best 5 alternatives for that slot with their reasons), **Change
  answers**. One line: "Built from published training research by the EG Plan Model. Not medical
  advice."

## Data and privacy

- Height, age and sex are saved in the profile (`S.body = { heightCm, birthYear, sex }`, stamped
  per field, synced to the person's own account only) so nutrition can reuse them and the
  questions open prefilled; weight goes in as a weigh-in. Never shown to friends, never in
  leaderboards. The other answers are kept with the plan's programme (`src.answers`) so "Change
  answers" opens with them.
- Nothing leaves the device or the person's own server.

## Testing

- The two friends as fixtures: A gets a 3 to 4 day upper/lower or full-body split with isolation
  work and RIR 1 to 2 after week 2; B gets 3 full-body days of mostly compound barbell or dumbbell
  lifts, RIR 3 to 4 in weeks 1 to 2, little isolation. Their exercise lists overlap by less than
  half. A Friend A with the same body numbers but female gets glutes and legs as default emphasis
  and shorter isolation rests, with the reason in the rationale; same safety rules.
- For a matrix of answers (days 2 to 5, minutes 30 to 90, each equipment, each level, each
  injury): every session fits its minutes; every exercise is in the catalogue, not `excluded`,
  available with the equipment, not stressing a flagged area; each muscle group hit at least
  twice a week; weekly sets within the level's range or capped by time; same answers + seed give
  the same plan; a different seed changes exercises but not the structure.
- The index: every exercise has a tier; every core exercise has a pattern; no stretch or "v. N" is
  core; counts per pattern × equipment so gaps are visible.
- Screens: the flow, the building moment, the plan page, Start, Shuffle, Swap.

## Review

`scripts/plan-model/review.mjs` writes `docs/emails-gym-planning/MODEL_REVIEW.md` for the coach:
every table in `plan-model.js` with its reasons, the core exercise list by pattern and equipment,
and the two friends' generated plans as worked examples. Plans show "Not reviewed by a coach yet"
until the model version is approved, as the templates do.

## Left to the builder

The exact pattern-inference keywords and overrides, the exact slot lists per session type, the
score weights' fine tuning against the test matrix, the wording of each rationale line, how the
swap sheet looks.

## Order of work

1. Exercise index and its review counts (the riskiest part: quality of picks).
2. Person + plan rules + builder, with the friend fixtures and the matrix tests.
3. Screens: questions, building, plan, start/shuffle/swap; remove T-017's matcher.
4. The coach's model review sheet.
