# Deliverable B — Email's Gym Training Methodology

## Principles and decision hierarchy

1. **Consistency before optimization (strong evidence, implementation principle):** sustainable training wins over an unattainable ideal.
2. **Specificity (strong):** heavy target-lift practice for strength, challenging volume and coverage for size, progressive aerobic work for fitness.
3. **Enough effort, not universal failure (moderate):** hypertrophy work often 1–3 RIR; beginners start 3–4; technical strength work 2–3. RIR is a perceived estimate.
4. **Rest protects output (moderate):** 2–4 min high-demand movements, ~60–120 s stable isolated work. Extend when performance is compromised.
5. **Progress from repeated data (product choice):** use transparent rep/weight rules; change one thing at a time.
6. **No magical split (strong/moderate):** choose training days based on availability, recovery and coverage.
7. **Safety is not overridden (strong):** pain/red flags pause progression and offer professional referral.

## Programming defaults

| Parameter | Default | Evidence tier / flexibility |
|---|---|---|
| Sessions | 2–4/week for starter plans | 2+ broad muscle exposures [1]; exact plan = product choice |
| Working sets | ~2–3/exercise per session; adjust to goal | Diminishing returns to total volume [1,2] |
| Hypertrophy | 6–15 reps compound; 8–20 isolation | Rep ranges practical convention, not strict science |
| Strength | 3–6 reps primary lifts (trained users), 2–3 RIR | Heavy-load specificity [1,4] |
| Beginner effort | First 1–2 weeks 3–4 RIR, low sets | Prudent conservative default |
| ROM | Comfortable controllable full ROM | General default; not obligatory under pain |
| Exercise prioritization | Skill-sensitive / goal priority first | Strength-order evidence [1] |
| Inter-session rest | Prefer ~48h between similarly hard exposures when practical | Convention; individualized |
| Program duration | 8 weeks | Product/default, not a biological cutoff |
| Deloads | Reactive reduction; week 8 optional/consolidation | Experimental evidence does not support universal full stop [6] |

## Session execution standard

Check symptoms, equipment and time; complete 5–8 min general warm-up and progressive lighter rehearsal for first demanding exercise. Warm-up sets are never tracked as prescribed work sets. Log every work set with actual load, repetitions and optional RIR; show prior matched exercise entry. The prescribed range is a target, not an instruction to compromise technique. Stop on sharp pain, dizziness, chest pain, syncope or concerning symptoms. For unusual symptoms, seek qualified clinical care rather than an app diagnosis.

## Weekly progression

For rep-range work, keep weight until all prescribed sets reach the range ceiling **with target RIR and acceptable technique**, then propose the smallest available increase next exposure. When one set falls short, hold weight and work toward stable reps rather than repeatedly jumping. Upper-body practical increments can be ~1–2.5%, lower-body ~2–5% where equipment permits; **these are software defaults, not validated physiological constants**. If smallest available jump is large, recommend more reps or a smaller alternative, not forced overload.

For strength lifts use submaximal technique-stable training (never automatic true 1RM testing) and moderate load changes only after repeated satisfactory exposures. For cardio increase duration first, then density/intensity; never automatically treat an age-based HR zone as exact. Do not advance intensity if the user reports symptoms or has a relevant risk condition.

## Volume management and individualization

Classify direct sets by primary muscle; optionally show a secondary 'estimated exposure' with indirect sets counted at 0.5 (inspired by the model in [2], **not** a personal growth prediction). Calculate per-muscle weekly distribution, compare to program intent, and flag outliers for manual review. Adding sets requires multiple weeks of tolerable workload and insufficient progress, not an algorithm blindly maximizing sets. Remove sets if repeated multi-exercise decline, poor adherence, or time strain occurs. Accept manual exercise swaps preserving movement and target tissues; store changes on the enrollment, never in the published template.

## Missed sessions, breaks and completion

Under 7 days without the next session: simply resume the next session in sequence rather than stacking. After 7–14 days away: take the next comparable exposure at 1 fewer set and 1–2 RIR farther from failure as a *conservative default*. More than 14 days: offer a 1–2 week re-entry mini-block and manual adjustment; do not infer fitness from calendar alone. Mark a week complete by sessions completed, not by calendar dates. Permit skip, repeat, change or pause enrollment with explicit user intent.

A beginner is ready to progress when they demonstrate repeatable controlled technique, complete at least 75% of planned sessions over the last 4 weeks, report recoverable stress and can handle the next program's time/equipment requirements. **The 75% threshold is an app convention.** No automatic medical clearance.

## Substitution rules

Match movement pattern and main target muscle first; then equipment, available loading, acceptable ROM and individual tolerance. Avoid equating barbell squat with knee extension for strength-skill progression. On substitution preserve relative effort prescription, not absolute load or estimated 1RM. Disallow substitutions the user marks avoid, and explain why an alternative was offered.

## Evidence and boundaries

Refer to Deliverable A for evidence grades and citations. These prescriptions apply to generally healthy adults and are not rehabilitation, pregnancy-specific, pediatric or clinical cardiovascular programs. Before publishing beyond a personal prototype, obtain review from a qualified S&C professional.
