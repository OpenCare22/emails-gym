# Deliverable A — Exercise Science Evidence Review

**Email's Gym | Research reconstruction | 10 November 2026**

## Scope and evidence grading

This document is a reconstructed, focused evidence review from the recovered Deep Research text supplemented with independent verification of the references below. It is **not** the original missing research deliverable or a systematic review with a reproducible literature-search protocol. Evidence labels: **high** = robust convergent synthesis/position statement, **moderate** = several studies but heterogeneous, **emerging** = preliminary/inconsistent or extrapolation, **product choice** = pragmatic app behavior, not a discovered physiological rule. Study populations are mostly generally healthy adults; advanced athletes and clinical populations are not interchangeable.

## Findings and practical conclusions

| Domain | Evidence and limitations | Confidence | Application |
|---|---|---|---|
| Resistance training | Improves strength, size and function across many feasible prescriptions [1,4] | High | At least 2 weekly exposures covering major muscles for general health |
| Hypertrophy volume | Increasing weekly set volume generally increases size gains, with diminishing returns [1,2]; no universally optimal number | Moderate–high | Start modest; build only when recovery, time and performance justify it |
| Volume counting | Fractional indirect sets fit the Pelland model better than simple total counting [2]; this is a modeling choice, not a validated individual dose meter | Moderate | Report both direct sets and approximate indirect exposure; don't display as clinical precision |
| Frequency | When volume is equal, hypertrophy frequency effect is small/inconsistent; strength may benefit from more practice [1,2] | Moderate | Select split primarily for schedule, set quality, technical skill and recovery |
| Loading | Many loading schemes grow muscle; heavier loads are especially useful for maximum 1RM strength [1,4] | High | Mostly 6–15 or 8–20 reps for muscle, 3–6 for trained strength lifts; numeric boundaries = defaults |
| Proximity to failure | Nearer failure associated with more hypertrophy in estimated-RIR analyses, but RIR estimation uncertain [3]; ACSM did not find consistently decisive failure benefits [1] | Moderate | Usually 1–3 RIR on work sets; do not force failure on high-skill lifts |
| Rest | Longer rests can help preserve repetitions; small pooled hypertrophy benefits above very brief rests [5] | Moderate | ~2–4 min for demanding lifts, ~60–120 s for isolation; adapt to performance |
| Exercise order | Earlier exercises may receive stronger strength adaptations [1] | Moderate | Place priority and skill-sensitive lifts early |
| ROM and lengthened bias | Full ROM supports general strength and mobility; lengthened partial enthusiasm exceeds clear universal evidence | Emerging for superiority | Full controllable ROM default; alternatives only by comfort and purpose |
| Deload | Controlled one-week full-cessation deload showed no hypertrophy benefit, potentially less strength progress [6]; not proof that all fatigue reduction is bad | Emerging | Use reduced hard sets/effort when needed rather than compulsory layoffs |
| Aerobic activity | WHO recommends 150–300 moderate or 75–150 vigorous min/week, plus strengthening [7] | High for health | Surface as health target, not a guarantee of optimal VO2max training |
| Concurrent training | The recovered report describes generally compatible cardio and lifting outcomes, with high-volume fatigue tradeoffs | Moderate, not independently verified here | Place intense cardio away from hard lower-body sessions if practical |
| HR zones / wearables | Age-based HRmax, optical HR and readiness scores are imprecise at the individual level | Moderate general caveat | Talk-test/RPE first; wearable data are supporting context, not override |
| Weight loss | Training helps body composition but energy balance is important; weight change cannot be promised by workouts alone | High as broad principle | Keep nutrition-related outcomes and training performance separate |

## Practical priorities by goal

- **Beginner/general fitness:** low barrier, repeatable whole-body training, technique and consistency before complexity.
- **Muscle growth:** adequate per-muscle hard sets; modest load or rep progression; exercise choices aligned with joints/equipment.
- **Max strength:** train the target lifts under progressively heavier but submaximal conditions, enough practice and rest.
- **Strength + size:** moderate-heavy compounds plus targeted accessories; assess both load and repetition trends.
- **Hybrid/conditioning:** build aerobic time gradually, use higher-intensity sessions selectively, respect recovery.
- **Time-efficient:** fewer quality sets, fewer exercises, sensible paired movements; don't claim the physiological optimum.
- **Fat loss/body recomposition:** emphasize adherence and muscle retention; nutrition is separately relevant.

## Disagreements and uncertainty worth retaining in the app

Volume-response curves are population averages, not a prescription for each individual; counted indirect sets are approximate; RIR is self-reported and more unreliable for novices; estimated 1RM is highly exercise-, rep- and technique-sensitive; muscle-length effects may vary by muscle/region; subjective soreness and device-based recovery should not trigger automatic reprogramming; the value of a calendar deload remains individual rather than universal. No program in this package has itself been clinically validated.

## Jeff Nippard public-source audit

**April 29, 2024 — “How many sets do you need?”** [8]. Public article links volume with training age and available time. Compatible with dose-response evidence; particular beginner/intermediate/advanced ranges are heuristics, not fixed thresholds.

**Essentials official description** [9]. Public emphasis on training efficiency, 2–5 training days, effort, exercise substitutions and tracking. These are reasonable product principles; claims about a specific minimum set dose should not be treated as guaranteed.

**Min-Max official description** [9]. Public low-volume/high-effort positioning. This is one feasible tradeoff but not evidence that universally reaching failure is required. Not copied here.

**Pure Bodybuilding official description** [9]. Public hypertrophy specialization, high effort, multiple splits and substitution options. Valid in broad strokes; don't overinterpret near-failure research as every set needing failure.

**Exercise rankings and lengthened partials.** The recovered report identifies these as public discussion themes. Universal letter-grade tiers are not scientifically validated across people; additional specific video dates, citations, and experiments need source-by-source audit before a claim-level video bibliography is published. **The Muscle Ladder** has not been reproduced or independently inspected here. The paid routines and all exercise combinations in our library are independently authored.
